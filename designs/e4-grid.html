01

Say you run a support agent.

It reads every incoming customer email, pulls up the order, decides refund, replacement or escalation, and drafts the reply. Today every one of those steps goes to a frontier model that was trained to do everything. Your agent only needs it to do this.

Cost

A small model specialised on one job runs on a fraction of the compute. Same work, a tenth of the bill.

Latency

Fewer parameters, shorter prompts, no queue behind the whole world. A reply in 200 milliseconds instead of four seconds.

Accuracy

It has seen a thousand of your tickets and your rules, not the whole internet. It stops guessing what you meant.

02

Meet the agent that builds it.

You tell it how the model should behave. It writes the eval and the training data, trains, and hosts the result. You never touch a GPU.

A new frontier model dropped? The agent re-runs your eval against it. If it wins, you train again. You do nothing.

04

Your data stays yours.

Training runs on your traces and never leaves your account. The model is yours to download, host anywhere, or hand to us to run. No shared pool, no cross-customer training.

05

Kullback is open source.

The harness that evaluates, trains and hosts is public. Read it, run it yourself, or let the agent run it for you. See Kullback.

06

Questions

Does it work for my workflow?

If your agent does the same kind of task thousands of times a week and you can say what a good answer looks like, yes. Bring one task and we evaluate it for free.

What happens when a better frontier model comes out?

The agent re-runs your eval against it the day it ships. If the new model wins on your tasks, you get a report. If yours still wins, nothing changes.

How long until it is live?

Evaluation in 48 hours. A trained, hosted model in about a week.
